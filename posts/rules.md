## What This Place Is For:

This space is for people who believe in open-source technology and want to work on it, promote it, and create art with it. Sharing things you've made is encouraged, especially code, insights, and resources. Promoting paid products or services is not what this space is for, unless they also happen to be open source.

## Rules:

> ### 1. Communication and Respect:
> • Don't direct offensive language at others.
> • Keep discussions focused on AI art and technology — no political or religious debates or content.
> • Art focused on political or religious subjects is okay so long as it's tasteful and not lazy — per moderators' subjective judgment.
> • Feedback is encouraged, but keep the tone supportive and constructive — don't dismiss or belittle others' work.
> • Don't share personal information without consent.
> • If you have an issue with another member, contact a moderator rather than escalating publicly.

> ### 2. Privacy, Sharing, and Credit:
> • All messages sent here are stored and publicly accessible — for LLMs to learn from and for people to find. To remove your messages from the public corpus, use the `/remove-from-hivemind` command with <@1316765722738688030>.
> • Anything posted here may be shared elsewhere unless the person who posted it has opted out. To change your sharing preferences, use the `/update_details` command with <@1316765722738688030>.
> • If someone has opted out, you'll see a "No Sharing" role on their profile — don't share their content publicly.
> • When sharing others' content, give proper attribution with a direct link to their work or profile.
> • If you share something that's sensitive from a copyright perspective, make sure you're comfortable being responsible for it in the unlikely event of a copyright claim.

> ### 3. NSFW Content:
> • NSFW content may only be posted in specifically designated NSFW channels.
> • No content involving minors or anyone who looks like they could be a minor. 18+ only.
> • No content featuring celebrities, politicians, or anyone without their explicit consent.

### A Note on Moderation:

Not everything that affects the quality of a community can be captured by strict rules alone, so moderators may sometimes need to use their own judgment to preserve the atmosphere of the community. We will always explain our reasoning on moderation decisions publicly in <#1475121919484366962> and aim to be as sparing and fair as possible.

## Norms:

These aren't hard rules, but this place will probably be better for everyone if you follow them.

> ### 1. Post in the right place:
> • Whatever you're doing, put it where it belongs: stuck right now → chatter for that topic, finished work → `resources` / `gens`, long builds → a thread, anything else → `off-topic`. For example, Minimax gens go in <#1533677158067736777>, not <#1532625331960152124>; LTX resources go in <#1457981813120176138>.

> ### 2. Share your progress, then present it well:
> • Dumping lots of excited progress updates is understandable and okay to a reasonable degree — this is a community for open experimentation and sharing, and raw work-in-progress is welcome here. But at some point, please consider taking a step back and presenting what you're doing well so others can understand it holistically: a short video or a write-up beats ten scattered messages.

> ### 3. Share how you did it:
> • Say what you ran, what you compared it to, and link it. Bigger the claim, bigger the proof. Avoid appeals to authority or confidence without proven substance.

> ### 4. Assume good faith of others:
> • Read it straight before you read it shady — terse replies, dumb questions, and parallel work included.
> • Link what you built on. Credit disputes go private with proof — side-by-side, diff, commit — or not at all, per Norm 3.

> ### 5. Disagree politely:
> • Critique the work, not the person. Say what's wrong specifically and what would fix it.
> • Avoid cruel or unnecessary sarcasm, mocking, or pile-ons. If you're steaming, step out and flag a mod in <#1475121919484366962> instead of replying, per Rule 1.

> ### 6. Ask bots for help before humans:
> • Respect people's time: if a generic AI chatbot can answer it, try that first. For domain-specific knowledge, ask in <#1163250319107555388> — the bot there answers from everything the community has figured out before, and it's typically good.
